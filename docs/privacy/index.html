Cosmo

Privacy

Last updated 4 November 2026

Cosmo is a free Windows app that sits at the top of your screen as a small island: music and lyrics, notifications, clipboard history, tasks, sticky notes, captures and more. It's made to keep your things on your PC. This page says what it keeps, what leaves your PC, and where it goes.

What stays on your PC

Settings, clipboard history, tasks, sticky notes, the shelf, saved lyrics and add-ons are kept in %APPDATA%\Cosmo. Clipboard history, tasks and sticky notes are encrypted so only your Windows account can read them. Text shortcuts are encrypted the same way. Notifications are shown and never saved. Screenshots and recordings go to Pictures › Screenshots › Cosmo. Uninstalling asks whether to remove Cosmo's data too.

Text shortcuts, if you make any: to notice a keyword being typed, Cosmo watches the keys you press in other apps while the feature is on. It remembers only the last few characters, in memory, forgets them when you switch windows or move the cursor, and never saves or sends them. Keys typed in Cosmo's own windows aren't watched. To put a shortcut's text in, Cosmo briefly borrows the clipboard and then puts back what was there.

Cosmo has no analytics, no ads and no tracking, and doesn't sell or share anything about you.

What goes out, and only for what you use

  • Lyrics: the song's title, artist and length go to LRCLIB, and to NetEase if LRCLIB has none.
  • Updates: every hour Cosmo asks GitHub for the newest version. You can turn this off. Add-ons are downloaded from GitHub only when you switch them on.
  • Weather, if you turn it on: your PC's location from Windows, rounded to about a kilometre, goes to Open-Meteo for the weather (or to MET Norway when Open-Meteo can't be reached) and to BigDataCloud for the place name. If you type a place instead, what you type goes to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim to find it, and Windows' location isn't used. Picking a place on a map opens one in your browser, drawn by OpenFreeMap; a place searched for there goes to Photon.
  • Sharing a capture: only when you choose Share, the capture is uploaded to Cosmo's sharing server. Anyone with the link can see it for 12 hours, then it's deleted.
  • Translation: text you translate goes through Cosmo's server to Cloudflare's AI and isn't kept.
  • Sync, if you sign in with Google: see below.

Sync with your Google account

Signing in is optional. If you do, Cosmo keeps your settings, clipboard history and its commands, text shortcuts, tasks and sticky notes the same on every PC you sign in on. You choose which of these sync.

What Cosmo asks Google for:

  • Your email address (openid, email), only to show which account you're signed in with.
  • Its own app folder in your Google Drive (drive.appdata): a hidden folder only Cosmo can use. Cosmo can't see, open or change any of your other files in Drive.

How it's kept: before anything is uploaded, Cosmo encrypts it on your PC (AES-256-GCM) with a sync password only you know. Google stores it but can't read it, and neither can the maker of Cosmo. Without your password the synced data can't be opened, and it can't be recovered.

Where it goes: straight from your PC to Google. It never passes through any other server, and the maker of Cosmo never receives your Google data, your email address or your password.

Signing out removes Google's permission and deletes the sign-in from your PC. To delete the synced data, open Google Drive on the web, then Settings › Manage apps › Cosmo › Options › Delete hidden app data, or take away Cosmo's access at myaccount.google.com/connections.

Cosmo's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. It's used only to sync your own data between your PCs, never for ads, never sold, and never read by a person.

Children

Cosmo isn't made for children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect anything from them.

Changes

If this page changes, the date at the top changes too. Changes that matter are mentioned in the release notes.
